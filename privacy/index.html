Privacy Policy

Tell Me One Story · Last updated: 24 October 2026

The short version

  • Your stories, your photos, and your book are kept on your phone. There is no account, and we keep no copy of your book, apart from any page or chapter you choose to report to us.
  • To write your pages, your answers are sent to our narrator service. It passes them to OpenRouter, which has them written only by Claude by Anthropic. Our service keeps none of your stories unless you report a page or chapter. OpenRouter says it keeps none either. Anthropic normally deletes them within 30 days (up to 2 years if its safety systems flag them), and none of these companies uses them to train AI.
  • Your photos are never sent to the narrator or to us.
  • If your phone's Android backup is on, Android can copy the app's data, including your stories and photos, to your own Google account. You control this in your phone's settings.
  • RevenueCat checks whether you have a subscription, and Google Play handles any payment. They never see your stories.
  • No ads, no tracking, nothing sold, and nothing is ever made public.

Who we are

Tell Me One Story is made by Francis Ivan Marie Adelan, an individual developer in the Philippines ("we", "us"). Under the Philippine Data Privacy Act of 2012 (Republic Act No. 10173), we are the personal information controller for the information described here. Francis is also our Data Protection Officer. For any privacy question or request, write to francisivan.adelan@gmail.com.

Why we use your information, and on what basis

  • To write your book. Before you begin, the app explains how your answers are used and asks you to agree. We use your first name, pronouns, year of birth if you give it, and your answers, including any health, faith, family or other sensitive details you choose to share, on the basis of that consent (Data Privacy Act, sections 12(a) and 13(a)). We use them to write and rewrite your pages and chapters, which includes the automatic safety screening by OpenRouter and Anthropic and OpenRouter's anonymous topic statistics described below. You can withdraw your consent at any time by no longer telling stories and using Delete everything. Withdrawing does not undo what was already sent to write your pages.
  • To run subscriptions. Checking and restoring a subscription is part of the service you choose (section 12(b)). Before you subscribe, it is our legitimate interest in knowing whether this install already has one (section 12(f)).
  • To review a page you report. Only when you tap Report and send it, on the basis of your consent.
  • To keep the service secure and working. Our legitimate interest in preventing abuse and fixing faults, through the short-lived technical logs described below.

You do not have to give your year of birth. Without your answers, the app cannot write your book.

What stays on your phone

Everything you create is stored in the app's private storage on your phone:

  • Your first name, pronouns, year of birth if you give it, your reminder time, and when you agreed to how your answers are used.
  • Your answers, the pages and chapters written from them (with each chapter's Threads), your corrections, the questions the narrator has written for you from your stories, whether a page showed crisis resources, and whether you reported it.
  • The "story bible": the people, places, and facts the narrator has noted from your answers, so your book stays consistent.
  • Your photos, which the app copies into its private folder. While you use the app, it also keeps temporary copies in its cache folder: of photos you pick or take, of archives you open to restore, and of every PDF and archive you save. Delete everything empties this folder, and Android may clear it sooner when space runs low.
  • A review code, if you entered one (only Google Play's reviewers have these).
  • A random ID that RevenueCat creates for this install.

This storage is protected by Android's app sandbox and your phone's storage encryption. We have no copy of it.

Android backup. Android can back up an app's data to your Google account and restore it when you set up a new phone, and can also copy it straight from your old phone to a new one. If backup is on, Android may include this app's data, including your stories and photos, though it stops backing up an app once its data passes 25 MB, which a book with photos can reach. The backup belongs to your Google account; we cannot see it. You can turn backup off in your phone's settings (for example under System, Backup, or Google, Backup, depending on your phone).

Delete everything in the app erases what it keeps for your book: your name and other details, your stories, pages, chapters, photos, story bible, reminder, any review code, and the app's cache folder. It does not cancel a subscription (do that in Google Play), and it does not remove RevenueCat's ID for this install or its subscription records (write to us for that). It also cannot reach copies that are already elsewhere: an Android backup in your Google account, files you saved or shared, a page you reported, or copies Anthropic still holds under the rules below. Uninstalling the app removes all of its storage from your phone.

What leaves your phone, and why

To write a page or a chapter. The app sends the following over an encrypted (HTTPS) connection to our narrator service:

  • Your first name, pronouns, and year of birth if you gave it.
  • The part of the book and the question, your answer, and any corrections.
  • When you correct a page, the page being rewritten.
  • Up to three earlier pages from the same part of your book, and the story bible.
  • For a chapter, the questions, answers, corrections, and pages from that part.

Your stories will mention other people. What you tell us about them is handled in the same way as the rest of your answers.

Our narrator service runs on Google Cloud in Singapore. It sends this to OpenRouter, which has it written only by Anthropic's Claude, and returns the text Claude writes. Our service does not store any of this content. Its own logs record only the time and kind of each request, whether it worked, how long it took, which AI model wrote it, how many AI tokens it used, and, if Claude declined, the category of the refusal. They contain none of your words.

OpenRouter, a US company, connects our service to Claude. Our service tells OpenRouter to send every request only to Anthropic, and OpenRouter's records show Anthropic serving our requests. We have checked our account settings: OpenRouter's options to log your prompts and the pages it returns, or to use them to improve its product, are off. With these settings, OpenRouter says it does not store your words. It does keep a record of each request (such as the model, token counts, cost and timing) for as long as it needs for its business. To screen requests for abuse, and to give requests a topic category for its public usage statistics, OpenRouter may run them through automated systems, which can include AI models it chooses; it says it stores those categories without your words and apart from account details. OpenRouter does not train AI on your content. See openrouter.ai/privacy.

Anthropic, which makes Claude, writes your pages. It receives your content from OpenRouter, as OpenRouter's provider. We have no contract with Anthropic ourselves; OpenRouter lists Anthropic's commercial terms as the terms for these requests. Anthropic's published policy for commercial use says it:

  • Deletes inputs and outputs within 30 days, except as below.
  • Keeps content its automated safety systems flag as breaking its usage policy for up to 2 years, and the safety scores for up to 7 years.
  • Keeps data longer where the law requires and, where its contract with OpenRouter allows, may keep anonymized data for research or statistics.
  • Does not use this content to train its AI models.

See Anthropic's Privacy Center at privacy.claude.com.

When the narrator sees a sign of danger. The narrator judges, automatically, whether an answer suggests you may be in danger right now. If it does, the app shows crisis resources with that page and notes this on your phone, and in any archive you save. Our service does not record it, and the app never contacts anyone on your behalf.

When you report a page. If you tap Report a problem and send it, the text of that page or chapter and your optional note are sent to our narrator service and stored on Google Cloud in Singapore so we can review what the narrator wrote. Nothing else from your book is sent, though the page itself usually includes your first name. The stored report holds no account or device ID, and no name other than any in the page itself, but like every request it appears for 30 days in Google Cloud's request logs, which record your IP address (see below). We delete reports once they are reviewed, and in any case about 12 months after they are sent.

Review codes. If you enter a review code, the app sends it to our narrator service to check it, and checks it again when the app starts and when you tell a story. Codes are for Google Play's app reviewers.

Subscriptions. The first stories are free. To keep going, you subscribe through Google Play, which handles the payment: we never see your card or account details. RevenueCat, our subscription service provider, confirms subscriptions for us:

  • Each time the app starts, and when you come back to it, it asks RevenueCat whether this install has a subscription. RevenueCat receives the random ID created for the install, the IP address the request comes from (which it uses to work out your country), the make and model of your phone, its Android version and language and region settings, your Google Play country, the app version, and when the app was last used. This happens even if you never subscribe.
  • If you subscribe, RevenueCat receives your purchase records from Google Play: the plan, the dates, the price and currency, the country of purchase, and the purchase reference. These let you restore your subscription on another phone.
  • RevenueCat keeps these records until we delete them: when you ask, or otherwise while the app is offered, so a subscription can be restored. It never receives your stories, pages, or photos. It handles this information for us as our processor, so this policy is the one that applies; RevenueCat's own policy is at revenuecat.com/privacy.

Our hosting provider. Google Cloud keeps a log of every request to our narrator service, including the check the app makes that the narrator is reachable each time you open the Keep tab. Each entry records the IP address it came from, the kind of software that sent it, the time, the address requested, and the result. These logs, and our service's own logs above, are kept for 30 days. Our service also holds IP addresses in memory, only while it is running, to limit how often each one can call it. Google processes these logs and any reported pages for us under its Cloud Data Processing Addendum.

Google Play. Google may share crash reports and usage statistics with us if you allow your phone to share usage and diagnostics data with Google. These do not include your stories.

Camera and microphone. The app asks to use your camera only when you tap Take a photo; those photos are kept like any other. The app does not use your microphone. If you speak an answer using your keyboard's microphone, your keyboard app handles the speech under its own privacy policy.

What we do not do

  • We do not show ads or use advertising IDs.
  • We do not include advertising, analytics or tracking tools in the app. The only statistics we see are subscription totals in RevenueCat, such as how many people start a trial, subscribe or cancel, and Google Play's crash and usage statistics.
  • We do not sell or rent your information, and we do not let anyone use it for their own purposes, apart from the limited uses by OpenRouter and Anthropic described above.
  • We do not make anything you write public, and there are no social features.

Passing your story on

You can save your book as a PDF or as a complete archive file. The PDF holds your name, your pages and chapters (with their Threads), and your photos. The archive holds everything the app keeps for your book: your answers, pages, chapters and photos, and also your pronouns, year of birth, reminder time, corrections, story bible, the questions still waiting for you, when you agreed to how your answers are used, and whether a page showed crisis resources or was reported. Photos keep any details your camera saved in them, which can include where a photo was taken. Where those files go, and who you give them to, is up to you. Keep the archive somewhere safe: it is a complete copy of your book and is not password-protected.

Daily reminder

The daily reminder is scheduled on your phone. It does not use a push-notification service or send anything to us.

Our website

This policy is published on our website, hosted by GitHub Pages. GitHub receives your IP address when you open a page. The website uses no cookies and no analytics, and its typeface is served from the website itself.

Your rights

Under the Data Privacy Act you have the right to be informed, to access, to object, to correct, to erase or block, to data portability, to claim damages, and to file a complaint with the National Privacy Commission. You can withdraw your consent at any time, as described above. Because your book lives on your phone, you can exercise most of these rights yourself:

  • See and correct your stories in the app. "That's not how it happened" rewrites a page with your correction.
  • Delete a photo, or everything, from within the app.
  • Take your data with you using the archive file.

For anything else, write to francisivan.adelan@gmail.com. Reports are not linked to an account, so to find one, include a few words of the page you reported. For subscription records, include your Google Play order number (it starts with GPA). If you believe we have mishandled your information, you can complain to the National Privacy Commission (privacy.gov.ph) or to the data protection authority where you live.

Children

Tell Me One Story is for adults aged 18 and over and is not directed to anyone under 18. The year of birth is optional; if one is entered that shows someone is under 18, the app will not accept it, but we do not otherwise check age. If you believe someone under 18 has sent us information, contact us and we will delete any report we can identify.

Security

Requests to our narrator service travel over HTTPS. The service stores no story content apart from pages you choose to report, and it limits how often it can be called. No system is perfectly secure, so please protect your phone with a lock screen and keep your archive files somewhere private.

Where your information is processed

We are in the Philippines. Our narrator service runs on Google Cloud in Singapore, where reported pages are stored; Google keeps the request logs and our service's logs in its worldwide logging service, so they may be stored outside Singapore. OpenRouter, Anthropic and RevenueCat are in the United States and process the information described here there; OpenRouter may also process it in other countries, and Anthropic may run Claude in any region where it has capacity. These countries may not protect your information in the same way as the law where you live. Google Cloud processes it for us under its Cloud Data Processing Addendum and RevenueCat as our processor under its terms. OpenRouter handles it under its terms, which include its data processing agreement, and Anthropic under its terms with OpenRouter; we have no agreement with Anthropic ourselves. Each company's policy is linked above.

Changes to this policy

If we change this policy, we will update the date above. If a change matters to how your information is used, we will tell you on the app's store listing or in an app update before it applies.
